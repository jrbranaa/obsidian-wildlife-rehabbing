## Use

Reference table for **formula amount per feeding** and **feeds per day** by age/weight,
for Western Gray, Eastern Fox, and California Ground Squirrels. Amounts are based on
% of body weight (5% through ~6 weeks, 6% from 6 weeks on) — a starting point, not a
hard rule; adjust to stool/weight trend per the notes below.

## Formula notes (Fox Valley 32/40 vs. 20/50)

- **32/40** = 32% protein / 40% fat. **20/50** = 20% protein / 50% fat.
- Switch from 32/40 to 20/50 **very gradually** once the squirrel hits ~100g — mix the
  two at a 2:1 ratio (20/50:32/40) and blend over several days, reducing the 32/40
  fraction daily, rather than switching outright.
- Some individuals do better staying on 32/40 longer — if a squirrel objects to or
  doesn't do well on 20/50, it's fine to stay on 32/40 a bit longer.
- This matches the operator's own blended-transition approach already in use (e.g.
  [[Abby]]'s gradual 32/40 → 20/50 blend).

## Weight gain benchmarks

- **Western Gray** (this chart's baseline species): 2–3 g/day at 2–3 weeks, 4–7 g/day
  at 4–6 weeks, ~8–10 g/day when older.
- **Smaller species** (e.g. fox squirrel, ground squirrel): ~2–4 g/day under 100g,
  5–6 g/day over 100g.
- Use these as a sanity check on whether a flat/slow-gaining animal is a feeding-volume
  problem or something else (GI issue, illness) — see e.g. [[OB]]'s case.

## Reading stool for over/under-feeding

- **Overfeeding**: soft stool — sometimes normal color, but often **white or greenish**.
- **Correct amount**: firm, light-to-dark gold, round or oblong pellets.
- This is a useful first check whenever a patient's stool goes soft/off-color before
  jumping straight to an infectious/parasitic differential — rule the simple
  volume/concentration explanation in or out first.

## Scheduling rules

- **Minimum 3.5 hours between feedings** once a squirrel is 3 weeks or older.
- Keep the schedule **consistent** in both timing and spacing.
- Older babies (6+ weeks), if healthy with no stool problems, can go up to **5.5–7%**
  of body weight (a bit over the standard 5%/6% baseline).
- **Douglas squirrels, flying squirrels, and chipmunks** run a faster metabolism —
  feed at **7%** (or a bit over) of body weight starting at 4–5 weeks, rather than the
  5–6% used for the species this chart is based on. Relevant to [[Clarice]]
  (Merriam's Chipmunk) — this chart's age/weight table is **not** directly applicable
  to her; use the 7%+ guidance and adjust frequency/volume by her actual
  age/weight/behavior rather than the table above.
- This chart is based on Western Grays specifically; smaller species may drop to less
  frequent formula feedings, move to solid food, and wean sooner than the table implies.

## Cautions

- All amounts are estimates — **watch stool and weight trend**, not just the table, for
  signs of under/over-feeding.
- Dedicated schedules for Douglas squirrels, flying squirrels, and chipmunks exist
  separately per the source chart's note — this table's numeric ranges are for
  Western Gray / Eastern Fox / California Ground Squirrel only.

## History

Log actual feedings in each patient's **## Feeding** table as usual; use this chart as
the reference for what volume/frequency is appropriate for a given age/weight, and to
sanity-check whether a stool change is more likely a feeding-volume issue before
reaching for an infectious/parasitic explanation.
