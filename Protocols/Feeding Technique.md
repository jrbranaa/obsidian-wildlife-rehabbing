How to hold, feed, and stimulate a squirrel. See [[Aspiration]] for what to do
if formula goes down the wrong way, and [[Formula Preparation]] for mixing.

## Holding & positioning

- Wrap in a baby blanket/soft cloth, head and front paws free (can cover the
  head entirely as long as you can see the mouth and confirm swallowing).
  Gloves (surgical/cotton gardening) are optional at this stage — squirrels
  can carry fleas/parasites.
- Hold in your lap on a towel/blanket/pillow; Kleenex underneath (they may
  eliminate before/after feeding). Keep cotton balls and extra Kleenex handy.
- Babies nurse on their stomachs: hold on the tummy, gently but firmly around
  the **back of the neck** (never the throat), thumb-and-forefinger "collar,"
  hand over the upper body.
- Syringe between first and second fingers, heel of hand on the plunger
  (gives more control than thumb-on-plunger); direct the nipple in, tilted
  slightly upward.
- **NEVER feed a squirrel lying on its back** — causes aspiration, which can
  cause pneumonia and death.

## Feeding pace

- Go slow, be patient — the nipple is unfamiliar. **"Push, pause; push,
  pause"** — release only about **0.05cc** at a time.
- To get a reluctant feeder started: jiggle the nipple in the mouth, or
  squeeze a drop onto the tip so he can smell/taste it. A little **Karo
  syrup** on the tip can help a very reluctant feeder start.
- **NEVER push hard on the plunger** — risk of aspiration.
- If he squirms/pulls away/pushes the nipple away: stop, wait for him to
  settle, try again. Use the pause to check formula temperature.
- Once accustomed, he'll knead with his paws and eventually hold the syringe
  himself, sucking hard — you may need to control the plunger so he doesn't
  eat too fast.
- Most babies close their eyes while sucking — **confirm he's awake and
  swallowing**, not just reflexively sucking, or he may choke.
- **Refusing the nipple for several feedings** = possible illness — investigate
  immediately. Try a different nipple type first.
- **Never feed more than the correct amount by weight** — check the stomach is
  nicely rounded, not taut/bloated. Overfeeding risks diarrhea/bloat and can
  be life-threatening (see [[Bloating]], [[Diarrhea or Soft Stool]]).
- Measure formula before/after feeding to log actual intake. Clean spilled
  formula off the squirrel (cotton ball/tissue, wet washcloth if needed) —
  left on the skin it causes abrasions/fur loss (see [[Milk Scald]]). Discard
  any formula left in the syringe.

## Stimulation

- After feeding, use a warm moist (or dry) cotton ball to gently stroke the
  genital area for urination/defecation. Q-tip works well for pinkies.
- Can take seconds to a couple minutes — be patient. Some squirrels need
  stimulation both before and after feeding.
- **Urine output should be ~2/3 of the fluid volume just given.**
- Wipe excretions off and dry the squirrel well afterward.

## Weighing & records

- Weigh at the same feeding time daily until **at least 8 weeks old**; after
  8 weeks, once or twice a week unless ill (daily if ill).
- Larger tree squirrels: ~5-6 g/day gain at 3-5 weeks, ~8-12 g/day older.
  Smaller species: 2-4 g/day, up to 6 g/day older. (Slightly higher than the
  Section-1 overview numbers in [[Feeding Schedule]] — use as a general
  cross-check, not a strict target.)
- Keep accurate feeding/medication/procedure records — required by SWR/DFW
  and useful to any rehabber the squirrel is later transferred to.
- Return the squirrel to a warm, covered basket after feeding — under ~4
  weeks, he'll likely fall asleep immediately.
