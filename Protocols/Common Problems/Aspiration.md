Squirrel babies sometimes aspirate while feeding — a nose/mouth-full of
formula drawn into the lungs. Directly relevant to [[Cass]]'s ongoing
aspiration-driven congestion case.

## Prevention

- **Never feed on the back** — hold on the stomach, around the shoulders, or
  sitting up leaning forward (older squirrels may prefer this).
- Most likely to happen **when feeding first starts** (ravenous eating).
- Check the syringe isn't sticking, the nipple hole isn't too big (delivers
  formula too fast), and nothing is leaking.
- If aspiration keeps happening: review feeding position, try a smaller
  syringe, slow down, use the push-pause pattern (see [[Feeding Technique]]).
  Fixing the technique usually resolves it.

## If it happens

- Stop feeding, wipe formula from the nose with a Kleenex, watch/listen for
  normal breathing (too fast, too slow, or abdominal breathing = abnormal).
- Recheck breathing every ~3 minutes for a while after.
- If in doubt, a small bulb syringe (squeezed halfway, released in one
  nostril at a time) can draw fluid out — may need repeating.
- **Severe signs** (gasping, open-mouth breathing, gurgling/choking): check
  for and clear any mouth/throat blockage. Holding upside down and patting
  the upper back can help.
- **Checking for pneumonia**: hold the squirrel's upper back to your ear —
  crackling/clicking = possible pneumonia (a stethoscope helps too). See
  [[Pneumonia]] — **Nuflor** is the manual's stated best treatment if
  diagnosed.
