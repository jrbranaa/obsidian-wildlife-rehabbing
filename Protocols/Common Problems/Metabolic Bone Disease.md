Fox Valley formula has adequate calcium — the risk period is **after
weaning/while weaning**, when solid-food calcium intake needs to be
sufficient. MBD weakens bones and prevents proper growth; caused by
calcium-deficient diet, but **calcium alone isn't sufficient** — prevention
beats treatment.

## Prevention

- **Sunlight** → Vitamin D → efficient calcium use for bone growth. Outdoor
  time as they age/wean matters for this reason beyond just acclimation. If
  indoor-only post-weaning, provide a **full-spectrum light source**.
- Solid diet centered on calcium/phosphorus-rich fruits & vegetables
  (especially green leafy veg — see [[Diet - Weaning Foods and Natural Foods List]])
  and nuts. Target **Ca:P ratio of 1 : 0.5–1**.
- A cuttlebone in the cage for gnawing.
- **Never** a diet restricted to sunflower seeds/peanuts — lacks the amino
  acids needed for calcium absorption.

## Recognizing it

Not climbing/jumping/active at the age expected → suspect MBD → get an X-ray
to confirm.

## Treatment

- Ground Tums tablet mixed with water, fed to the squirrel.
