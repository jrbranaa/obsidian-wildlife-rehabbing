Reference for identifying/sizing the squirrel species this operation typically
receives. El Dorado County has 7 squirrel species; SWR usually rehabs 6 of
them (all but the Golden-Mantled Ground Squirrel, found near South Lake Tahoe
and higher elevations — notable given [[Clarice]], a Merriam's Chipmunk
believed to have hitchhiked down from the Tahoe area, is *also* outside this
area's normal species range).

| Species | Adult weight | Markings / notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Western Gray Squirrel** | 340–964 g (1.5–2 lbs) | Most abundant locally. Silver-gray back/legs, white underside, white eye-rings, long bushy tail. Color phases include black (melanistic) and rare pure white. |
| **Fox Squirrel** | Smaller than Western Gray | Introduced to the West in 1904 (native to the East). Grayish-brown back/head, tan-to-rust face/legs/feet/underparts, tail underside tan/rust to bright orange. Occasional white markings. More frequently melanistic than Grays. |
| **Douglas Squirrel** (Chickaree / Pine Squirrel) | 250–300 g | ~1/4 the size of the large tree squirrels. Very vocal, energetic. Gray-to-brown fur, dark brown side stripe, beige face/underside markings, medium flat tail. Elevations 2,500–6,000 ft. |
| **California Ground Squirrel** | 454–625 g (1–1.4 lbs) | Usually a bit smaller than Western Gray. Stippled gray/light-brown fur, V-shaped whitish shoulder mantle, white eye-rings, buff underparts, shorter straighter tail than Gray. Colonial, ground burrows up to 200 ft long / 30 ft deep. |
| **Northern Flying Squirrel** | 75–150 g | Very small, infrequently seen. Silky gray/cinnamon-brown fur, wide feathery tail, patagium (gliding membrane) wrist-to-ankle, glides up to 300 ft. Enormous eyes — only nocturnal squirrel. |
| **Chipmunks** (several CA species) | 2.5–5 oz (~70–140 g) at maturity | Light-to-dark brown with black/white striping. Mainly terrestrial but can climb. Mountainous-region nesters (burrows, tree cavities, logs, stumps). |

## General principle

Raise any baby squirrel **with its own kind** — social skills for survival are
learned through play/interaction with other squirrels. Litters typically run
2-4; pair up singletons with another single baby of **similar age** as soon as
possible. **Don't pair weaned juveniles with much-younger ones (>2 weeks
apart), or different species.** Consult an experienced rehabber if unsure.

See [[Development Stages]] for age/weight/feeding/housing by stage, and
[[Intake Procedure]] for what to do in the first hours after a squirrel is
received.
