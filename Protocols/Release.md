## Release Cage (12-16 week stage)

- Located near/under trees. Regulation size: **8' L x 8' W x 8' H minimum**
  (many built larger), wood + ½" wire/hardware cloth.
- Wooden nest box filled with hay/leaves/safe bedding + blanket pieces for
  warmth, plus feeding/training furnishings.
- Small (~3" round/square) lockable "release door" near the top, adjacent to
  an outdoor tree branch.
- Entire 6-8 ft trees and long thick limbs/branches for climbing.
- Purpose: predator-proof space to build climbing/jumping stamina, acclimate
  to outdoor conditions and local wildlife, learn predator recognition,
  compete for food (ideally **2+ housed together**), and practice
  nest-maintenance.
- Feed 2x/day (early morning + mid-late afternoon — squirrels are diurnal),
  heavy on fruits/greens/veg/nuts/seeds and natural foods (see
  [[Diet - Weaning Foods and Natural Foods List]]).
- **Check daily at feeding** even though he should be avoiding contact by
  now — confirm activity, no illness/injury, eating well. Recapture (mesh
  bird net) and treat if something's wrong — rare once in the release cage,
  but happens.

## Release age/weight thresholds

| Group | Release age | Release weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Larger tree squirrels (Gray, Fox) | 14-16 weeks | well over 450g |
| Smaller species (Douglas, Flying, chipmunks) | as early as 2.5-3 months | 150-300g |

## Site criteria

- Good tree cover, mixed fresh vegetation, native-tree acorns/nuts, fruit,
  berries, fungi, insects, and a reliable water source (creek, or a large
  bowl freshened daily).
- **The release location must already have this species living in the
  area** — directly relevant to [[Clarice]] (Merriam's Chipmunk, likely a
  Tahoe-area hitchhiker not native to this immediate area): her eventual
  release can't be at a standard local site, needs a location where her
  species actually occurs.
- Release **very early in the day** (diurnal species, gives a full day to
  acclimate).
- Avoid: areas over-populated by squirrels (territorial conflict, especially
  between species), commercial crop areas, high-traffic areas, or anywhere
  the species is treated as a pest (illegal to shoot/trap/poison locally, but
  it happens).

## Readiness signs

Sleek coat, well-filled-out (even fat) body, full bushy tail. Fast/accurate
climbing/jumping/running in all directions. Heavy play with nest-mates
(wrestling, pouncing, exploring, grooming). Independent, resistant to
handling, may try to escape the cage. Eating large amounts of fruit/veg/nuts
and natural foods; drinks from a bottle or laps; can self-crack most nuts
(may still need help with harder ones like walnuts).

## After release

Continue providing daily fruit/greens/veg/nuts/water for up to **2 weeks**
post-release, or as long as he keeps returning to the cage/nest box. Larger
tree squirrels fully "wild up" around **9-11 months** and often disperse to
new territory (especially males).

See [[Development Stages]] for the age progression leading up to this stage,
and [[Handling Adults]] for handling an adult intake (distinct from a raised
juvenile at release time).
