What to do in the first hours after receiving a newly-rescued squirrel. See
[[Development Stages]] for stage-specific detail once past intake, and
[[Feeding Schedule]] for the ongoing accelerated/conservative ramp-up to full
formula.

## Step 1 — Warm first, always

Assume every rescued squirrel is in at least **mild shock** (temperature drop)
and vulnerable to pneumonia/respiratory problems. Keep warm and dry
regardless of age or ambient temperature.

- **Never feed a "cold" squirrel** — hydrate and warm first.
- Basket with a snap lid, or a secure well-ventilated box, lined with several
  thick cloth layers (fleece/flannel baby blankets, t-shirt/sweatshirt
  material — **not terry cloth**, nails catch in the loops).
- Dark, quiet location, away from traffic/noise, on a heating pad set on
  **Low**. Wrap the animal warmly while feeding.
- Cover the basket top/sides with a larger blanket/towel to block drafts and
  add a sense of security.
- Squirrel should feel **"toasty warm"** to the touch. Stay on the heating pad
  continuously if 6 weeks or younger, still in shock, injured, ill, or if the
  room is cold; once warmed, can move to half-on/half-off the pad so he can
  self-regulate. Room temp target: **72–75°F**.
- **Homeopathics** (optional, can't hurt): **Arnica** (injuries/bruising),
  **Aconite** (fear/stress), or "Rescue Remedy" (sprayed on blankets, not
  directly on the squirrel) — usable immediately on intake.

## Step 2 — Weigh

Once warm and calmed, weigh him (tare a small basket/bag/sock first). Weight
determines: approximate age, fluid/formula amount, medication dosing, and
overall health trend. Use a gram scale (0.5–1000g) if possible.

## Step 3 — Fluid therapy (before any formula)

Assume **all** rescued squirrels are somewhat dehydrated and in some shock.

- **Dehydration check**: pinch skin between the shoulder blades — no rebound
  within 1-2 seconds = dehydrated. Also watch for sunken eyes, dry mucous
  membranes, absent/scant urination or bowel movements, or small hard dark
  droppings.
- **First 6-12 hours** (age/circumstance dependent): **LRS or Electrostat
  only** — no formula, no solid food. LRS can be given PO or SubQ; Electrostat
  PO only. An injured/ill squirrel unwilling to take fluid orally needs **LRS
  SubQ** — faster but more invasive.
- **Fluid volume rule of thumb**: **7-8% of body weight** in fluids for the
  first couple of feedings (up to **10%** if severely dehydrated), then back
  down to **5%**.

## Step 4 — Introduce formula, gradually

Once hydrated, introduce Fox Valley formula gradually per [[Feeding Schedule]]
(accelerated vs. conservative ramp-up), increasing strength if no soft
stool/diarrhea appears. If in doubt about how much to hydrate, **use the
conservative schedule.**

For a weaned/adult squirrel: after hydration, try liquid food first (Ensure,
optionally blended with a little berry yogurt or applesauce), working up to
solids if he can self-feed.
