source: Sierra Wildlife Rescue Squirrel Manual (2025), by Nan Powers and Linda Mueller
---

- Keep **tetanus vaccination current** — squirrel teeth are sharp and strong;
  a preliminary tetanus shot is worthwhile for anyone handling wildlife
  regularly.
- Squirrels are **not zoonotic** in the usual sense — they don't normally
  transmit disease to humans. Rabies is extremely rare in tree squirrels and
  very seldom seen in ground squirrels.
- Some tick/flea-transmitted diseases (e.g. **Bubonic Plague**) can be
  carried by squirrels, particularly adults — outbreaks are tracked by
  Public Health/CDC and SWR is notified when relevant. **Know where and under
  what circumstances a squirrel was found**, and watch for signs of severe
  illness or strange behavior early.
- **Treat any bite from a squirrel seriously** — clean and use a topical
  antibiotic; if there's any sign of a zoonotic illness in the animal, it may
  need testing and the handler may need medical consultation/treatment.
