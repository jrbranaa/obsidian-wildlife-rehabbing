> [!warning] Verify before use
> From the SWR (May 2026) squirrel dosage chart. Confirm concentration, dose,
> and use-case with your supervising vet before relying on this.

## Use

Two distinct uses on this chart:

1. **PO, once** — systemic flea/external-parasite treatment (standard Capstar use).
2. **Topical, as needed** — diluted and used to flush/treat **maggot-infested
   wounds (myiasis)**. This is an off-label rehab technique: nitenpyram kills
   fly larvae in a wound on contact. Confirm dilution and technique with your
   supervising vet before using it this way.

## Dosing

- **1 mg/kg**.
- Prepared per the chart by crushing and dissolving **1 tablet** to reach
  **1.14 mg/mL** — confirm the tablet strength used to make that dilution
  matches what you have on hand.

### Dose from weight (at 1.14 mg/mL, per SWR chart)

| Weight | Dose (1 mg/kg) |
| ------ | --------------- |
| 50 g   | 0.04 mL |
| 100 g  | 0.09 mL |
| 200 g  | 0.18 mL |
| 300 g  | 0.26 mL |
| 500 g  | 0.44 mL |
| 1000 g | 0.88 mL |

## Cautions

- PO dosing is for external parasites, not a GI dewormer/coccidiostat — it has
  no activity against coccidia or intestinal parasites.
- For the topical/wound-flush use, avoid mucous membranes and keep it out of
  the eyes and mouth.

## History

Log each dose/application in the patient's **## Medications** table
(drug = `Capstar`, route = `PO` or `topical`).
