Age-by-age guide to appearance, feeding, and housing. Cross-check against
[[Feeding Schedule]] for the age/weight/volume/frequency table; this note adds
developmental milestones and housing progression. Smaller species (flying
squirrels, Douglas squirrels, chipmunks — relevant to [[Clarice]]) develop
faster, wean sooner, and need a higher formula % per feeding (6-7% from the
start, vs. 5% baseline here) — see [[Feeding Schedule]]'s scheduling rules.

## Newborn – 10 days (neonate / "pinky")

**Not for novice rehabbers** — specialized care.
- **Weight**: 10-20g. Pink, transparent skin, no fur, eyes tightly closed and
  "bulgy," ears glued to head. Umbilical cord/remnant attached for first 10
  days. Sleeps except when feeding. Chills easily — supplemental heat
  **85-90°F**.
- **Feeding**: 0.5-1cc every 1.5-2h, 24h/day. Wrap and keep warm while
  feeding; stimulate every feeding. Urine clear/pale yellow. Pre-formula stool
  dark brown/firm; once on formula, dark gold, firm, round is the target
  texture until solids.
- **Housing**: Covered basket, snap-lid, soft ravel-free bedding, heating pad
  Low to maintain 85-90°F internal. Room temp 80-85°F. A warm wet washcloth
  over the basket (under the towel) or a small wet sponge in an open jar in
  the basket corner helps humidity for furless pinkies.

## 10 days – 2 weeks (infant)

- **Weight**: 15-35g. Still pink, faint dark spine streaks where fur will
  start. Eyes closed but more slitted. Umbilical cord fully gone.
- **Feeding**: 1-1.5cc every 2h. Once cord is off, can drop the deep-night
  feed, but keep at least one late-night (11pm-12am) and early-morning
  (5-6am) feed; add a 3am feed back if weakened/sick/not nursing well.
- **Housing**: Same as above — still chills easily, still needs stimulation.

## 2-3 weeks (older infant)

- **Weight**: 30-60g. Turning bluish-gray and fuzzy except underside; eyes
  less bulgy, more obvious slits.
- **Feeding**: 1.5-3.0cc every 2.5-3h day and evening, including one
  mid-morning (~4am) feed until over 60g/3 weeks.
- **Housing**: Heating pad Low under the basket. Clean every few days,
  leaving some old bedding. **No chlorine bleach or scented products** —
  scents irritate mucous membranes; use hot water + unscented/gentle
  detergent.

## 3-4 weeks (baby)

- **Weight**: 60-90g. Gray/furry except underside; white chest fur starting.
  Eyes still closed but slits more obvious. Lower incisors emerging — may
  see a little blood on the syringe from teething.
- **Feeding**: 3-4.5cc every 3.5h.
- **Housing**: Same as 2-3 weeks.

## 4-5 weeks (older baby, eyes closed)

- **Weight**: 90-130g. Gray topside, white chest fur, furry all over by ~5
  weeks. Eyes beginning to blink/"wink" — should open around 5 weeks.
  Starting to explore, "Velcro stage" (grabs/lurches when picked up).
  **Do not expose to bright light/camera flash for several days after eyes
  open** — can damage them.
- **Feeding**: Switch to **2:1 formula ratio** from here on. 4.5-6.5cc every
  4h.
- **Housing**: Same, heating pad Low under one end only so he can
  self-regulate.

## 5-6 weeks (older baby, eyes fully open)

- **Weight**: 130-150g. Furry all over, eyes/ears open, tail curls a little.
  Vision clears a few days after eyes open. Cutting upper incisors. If urine
  is dark/strong-smelling, he's being under-stimulated — needs it at least
  2x/day still.
- **Feeding**: 6-7.5cc every 4-4.5h.
- **Housing**: **Move to a small cage** (~18"-2' W x 2' H, ½" 14-16 gauge
  welded wire) once he can focus (a few days after eyes open). Washable
  carpet/bath mat (no loops), hammock made from a blanket hooked/clothes-pinned
  to the wire (start low, raise as climbing improves), hamster-size water
  bottle, cuttlebone. Cover cage top (predator security). Drop daytime heating
  pad use unless recovering from illness; keep it on Low at night under one
  end. After a few days, add thick climbing branches (oak/pine/cedar/fir/
  maple/aspen) and gnaw wood; add 3-4" Timothy/Orchard hay across the floor,
  changed frequently.

## 6-7 weeks (older baby → juvenile)

- **Weight**: 150-200g. Furry everywhere incl. tail underside/abdomen. Upper
  teeth more evident, cutting molars, shredding/gnawing more. Standing/
  walking steadier, may attempt climbing/jumping. Vocal/chattery. **Watch cage
  security closely at feeding time** — escape risk rises here.
- **Feeding**: 7.5-10cc, 4x/day. Start introducing dry diet (Zoopreem or
  homemade squirrel chow) — rub with apple/grape/walnut/blueberry/yogurt if
  he won't nibble at first. Once nibbling, add 1-2 pieces soft fruit
  (avocado, watermelon, banana, peach) + 2 pieces dry food/day.
- **Housing**: 2' W x 3' H cage, same furnishings as above.

## 7-9 weeks (pre-weaned juvenile)

- **Weight**: 200-300g. Longer fur, bushy tail. Active — walking, climbing,
  jumping, running. Drinks from water bottle. Teeth fully developed.
- **Feeding**: 12.5-18cc, 3-4x/day. **Start actively weaning once down to
  3x/day.** Dry diet available at all times. Add firmer fruit/veg (peeled
  apple, large berries, pear, large grapes, broccoli, carrot, brussels
  sprouts) and large shelled nuts/half-nuts or large cracked acorns — always
  peeled/large pieces (choking risk), introduce new foods slowly, one or two
  at a time. **No highly acidic fruit yet (oranges etc.), no salted nuts, no
  peanuts/sunflower seeds** (risk of Metabolic Bone Disease from an
  amino-acid-poor diet — see [[Metabolic Bone Disease]]).
- Check incisor alignment now — uppers straight down, lowers slightly
  curved in, meeting evenly; misalignment matters since incisors grow
  continuously.
- **Housing**: Same cage. Start acclimating outdoors (safe, predator-free
  spot) in similar-temp weather; bring in if temp drops >5°F or after a
  couple hours at first, extending gradually. Nest box (attached high) may
  be preferred over hammock at this stage.
- **Handling**: Begin reducing handling to feeding-time only. Never let him
  free-roam a room or climb on you as a "tree" — habituation risk. Keep
  domestic pets away entirely. Introduce him to heavy gloves now (before he's
  big enough to need them for safety).

## 9-11 weeks (weaning juvenile)

- **Weight**: 300-420g. Longer/denser coat. Very active — playing, wrestling,
  pouncing, hanging upside down, grooming more.
- **Feeding**: 18-25cc. Reduce formula to 2x/day as solid-food interest
  grows. Some self-wean as early as 9 weeks; **cut off formula by ~3 months**
  regardless, before Release Cage. Thinning the formula (more water) can
  nudge a reluctant weaner toward solids.
- **Housing**: 2-3' W x 3-4' H cage, hammock/nest box high up, hay + carpet.
  Outdoor daytime placement if safe/predator-free from this stage on
  (spring/summer/fall); indoors or protected space (garage/shed) overnight/
  winter.

## 11-12 weeks (weaned juvenile)

- **Weight**: 420-450g+. Longer fur, bushier tail. Very active, increasingly
  independent, may dislike handling.
- **Feeding**: 3-5 pieces chow/day plus fruit/veg/nuts/greens; may still
  nurse 1-2x/day (~20-25cc/feed) until fully self-weaned (anytime 9-12 weeks,
  signaled by eating less formula/refusing it). If not self-weaned after 8-10
  days on one formula feed/day, dilute by +25% water daily until refused.
  Small species (e.g. Douglas) wean earlier/smaller (~120-140g).
- **Handling**: **Stop handling except when absolutely necessary; stop
  talking to him** — accelerates the "wilding up" process.

## 12-16 weeks (growing up, becoming wild)

Larger tree squirrels aren't considered capable of nest-building, predator
evasion, or independent foraging until **14-16 weeks** — this stage happens
in a **Release Cage** (see [[Release]]) for ~3.5-4 weeks pre-release.
